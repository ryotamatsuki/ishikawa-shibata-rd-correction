## Primary target

**International Journal of Industrial Organization (IJIO).**

Rationale:

- IJIO is the official journal of EARIE and explicitly covers theoretical industrial organization, strategic behavior, market structure and technological change.
- The manuscript is a pure theoretical-IO correction involving oligopoly, R&D, asymmetric spillovers and competition/cooperation thresholds.
- IJIO published in January 2026 a directly relevant third-party correction-style contribution, `Generic entry, price competition, and market segmentation in the prescription drug market – a comment`, which revises a theoretical condition in a previously published IJIO article. This is strong evidence that a compact theory comment is an existing editorial pathway rather than an invented article concept.
- IJIO is currently a subscription journal. Elsevier states that authors in hybrid/subscription journals may publish under the subscription model at no APC cost.
- The present note is approximately 3,520 main-text words, three propositions, one figure and a short appendix. No current IJIO-specific word cap inconsistent with this length was found.

## Primary article type

**Working article type: `Comment`.**

Evidence: a 2026 IJIO publication is explicitly titled `... – a comment` and its abstract describes itself as a note revising the theoretical condition in an earlier IJIO article.

Control: the current public IJIO Guide-for-Authors taxonomy could not be reliably retrieved in this audit. Therefore the substantive `Comment` pathway is verified by current journal precedent, while the exact submission-portal dropdown label remains `STAGE5_PORTAL_CONFIRMATION_REQUIRED`. This is treated as an administrative confirmation, not a substantive journal-fit blocker.

## Backup 1

**Review of Industrial Organization.**

This is the strongest fee-free fallback: direct industrial-organization scope, theoretical contributions, hybrid publishing with a subscription route for which Springer states no APC applies, LaTeX support, and historical publication of comment-form articles. Its weakness relative to IJIO is that a current 2026 third-party mathematical-comment article-type pathway was not verified as directly as at IJIO.

## Backup 2

**International Review of Economics & Finance.**

IREF has the strongest original-journal and original-reader advantage and has published a third-party `Comments on ...` article. It is nevertheless ranked behind RIO because Elsevier currently lists IREF among its fully gold open-access economics/finance journals. A mandatory APC therefore applies absent a waiver/agreement, which conflicts with the project's preference for a zero-mandatory-fee route. Exact current IREF APC amount was not verified from an authoritative journal-specific primary page during Stage 4 and must not be invented.

Operational fee-free fallback after RIO: **Journal of Economics**, which is hybrid with a no-APC subscription route and has historical comment precedent.

## Journals not selected

### Economics Letters

Current official scope states that comments or pedagogical notes are not suitable and that contributions are usually limited to 2,000 words excluding references. The present 3,520-word third-party correction is therefore structurally mismatched without destructive compression and a change of article concept.

### Journal of Economics

Good mathematical-theory fit and a verified no-APC subscription route, with historical comment precedent. It ranks below IJIO/RIO because the direct industrial-organization audience is weaker.

## Main rejection risks

1. Moderate editorial-significance risk because the equilibrium formulas survive.
2. Moderate narrowness risk because the note corrects one paper rather than adding a new model.
3. Administrative uncertainty about the exact live portal article-type label and any submission fee.

The first two are mitigated by exact analytic zero-set corrections and a strict positive-measure classification consequence. They should not be mitigated by adding new economics.

## Why this ordering

`IJIO > RIO > IREF > Journal of Economics > Economics Letters` follows manuscript-journal fit rather than prestige alone. IJIO uniquely combines current comment precedent, exact theoretical-IO audience and a zero-APC subscription route. RIO preserves subject and fee fit but has weaker current comment-path evidence. IREF has the original audience and third-party comment precedent but currently imposes an open-access cost structure. Journal of Economics is a sound mathematical fallback with less direct audience. Economics Letters is explicitly unsuitable for comments and too short for the present note.
