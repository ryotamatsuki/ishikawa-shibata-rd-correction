# Editorial Risk Audit — IJIO

Primary target: International Journal of Industrial Organization.

Risk labels are qualitative (`LOW`, `MODERATE`, `HIGH`), not acceptance probabilities.

## Editorial-necessity test

| Test | Assessment | Reason |
|---|---|---|

No `FAIL` or `WEAK` item.

## Desk-reject simulation D1--D10

### D1 — Too minor: “The equilibria remain valid. Why publish this?”
Risk: **MODERATE**.
Response: the note corrects explicit published general threshold statements; two exact zero-set results and an open-set qualitative classification reversal show the issue is structural rather than typographical.
Mitigation: keep Proposition 2 geometry and Proposition 3 positive-measure consequence prominent; do not add a new model.
Residual risk: some editors may still regard the correction as narrow.

### D2 — Too old: “The target paper is from 2021. Why now?”
Risk: **LOW–MODERATE**.
Response: mathematical validity is not time-limited; the correction remains useful while the paper is citable and its thresholds can be reused. The note does not manufacture urgency.
Manuscript change: none.

### D3 — Too narrow: “One paper, one threshold issue.”
Risk: **MODERATE**.
Response: the issue propagates through three named observations/equation groups and changes a positive-measure classification region. A Comment is precisely the appropriate narrow vehicle.
Journal implication: favors IJIO's demonstrated Comment pathway.

### D4 — Comment policy: “We do not publish comments.”
Risk: **LOW** substantively, **administrative confirmation remains**.
Response: IJIO published `Generic entry, price competition, and market segmentation in the prescription drug market – a comment` in Volume 104, January 2026. Exact live portal label must still be checked.

### D5 — Significance: “The corrected curves differ only slightly numerically.”
Risk: **MODERATE**.
Response: magnitude is not the theorem. At lambda=0, gamma>=1, every regular asymmetric point on the asserted constant-sum equality line fails equality, and an admissible point reverses qualitative classification on an open neighborhood.
Manuscript change: retain analytic argument before figure; figure remains illustrative.

### D6 — Existing result: “Original Proposition 2 already says preferences depend on gamma.”
Risk: **MODERATE**.
Response: that general dependence statement is not the error. The correction targets later explicit gamma-invariance/scalar-threshold characterizations and provides the corrected zero-set geometry. The note should present original Proposition 2 as a consistency point, not an error.

### D7 — No welfare impact
Risk: **LOW**.
Response: this is deliberately not a welfare-correction claim. Published aggregate-R&D/social-preference classification is a legitimate theoretical object requiring correction on its own terms.

### D8 — No empirical consequence
Risk: **LOW**.
Response: IJIO publishes theoretical work. A mathematical Comment need not alter an empirical estimate when it corrects a published theoretical classification.

### D9 — Technical burden: “Too much algebra for the payoff.”
Risk: **LOW–MODERATE**.
Response: main text contains only the decisive identities; longer certificates are relegated to four short appendices and exact code. The three-proposition architecture is already compressed.

### D10 — Better as author corrigendum
Risk: **MODERATE**.
Response: the submitting author is not an original author and cannot issue an author corrigendum. A third-party Comment is a distinct editorial object, and IJIO has a recent example of that route.
Contact implication: no original-author contact is required as a precondition for direct submission.

## Main desk-reject risk

**Editorial significance / narrowness (D1 + D3).**

The correct mitigation is disciplined framing: exact analytic correction + positive-measure classification consequence + explicit surviving results. Expanding into welfare, a new model, downstream citation effects or extra simulations would weaken rather than strengthen the submission.

## Journal-specific conclusion

IJIO survives the desk-reject simulation. No risk requires a substantive Stage-4 manuscript expansion. The residual risks are ordinary editorial judgment and live-portal administration, not mathematical defects.
