## Policy conclusion

Current Elsevier journal policy requires authors who used generative-AI/AI-assisted tools in manuscript preparation to include a separate declaration immediately before the references. It also states that AI use in the research process, including code development or data analysis, should be described in the Methods section or a suitable alternative section. AI tools cannot be authors. Authors remain responsible for accuracy, integrity and the final submitted text.

This project has substantive AI-assisted workflow provenance and therefore should **not** rely on the copy-editing exemption.

## Proposed manuscript-preparation declaration

Title:

**Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process**

Working text:

> During the preparation of this work, the author used OpenAI ChatGPT to assist with manuscript structuring and drafting, revision and language editing, and preparation/review of reproducibility code and publication-workflow materials. After using this tool, the author reviewed and edited the content as needed and takes full responsibility for the content of the publication.

This wording follows the current Elsevier recommended declaration structure but must be fact-checked and explicitly approved by the author before submission.

Because AI assistance also entered the mathematical/reproducibility workflow, a separate short statement should appear in the reproducibility/methodological text, for example:

Do not describe the AI system as an author, mathematical authority, independent verifier, or source of evidence. The canonical evidence is the explicit algebra, exact verification scripts, and human-author responsibility.
